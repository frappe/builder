# New page or full redesign

The flow follows Impeccable's new-work process (github.com/pbakaus/impeccable, Apache-2.0), on its code-led path.

## 1. Decide what is already true
Settle the case first; it decides how much you invent.
- **Sibling page in an established site.** Unless the user asked for a different look, check whether the site already has designed pages (query_records 'Builder Page', excluding the open one; published and recently modified first). If it does, read_page one to three of them, the home page first (get_document('Website Settings') names it), plus the tokens (query_records 'Builder Token') and components. The visual system is then fixed: reuse its exact fonts, palette handles or hexes, header and footer components ({el: div, component: <id>}) and section rhythm. Only this page's structure and content are yours. The first page you read is the primary reference downstream, so read the page to match first; when the user named a page, that page alone is the reference. When the site's pages visibly disagree, ask one question, which page's look to follow (the pages as options), with a fresh look as the last option. A reference whose pages move carries its motion: name the class hooks its scripts need in the brief, and attach_page_script its scripts after the build.
- **Redesign.** Keep the page's facts, content, function and brand commitments; replace its visual world rather than polishing it. The old look tells you what the subject is, not what it becomes.
- **No visual authority** (an empty site, or the user asks for a new look): create a new world, step 3.
- **An attached image of a page design** is the approved direction: derive the palette, type and layout from it, carry its url in the brief as REFERENCE IMAGE: <url>, and go to step 4. An attached photo of a real subject (their product, shopfront, portrait) is content to place: HERO IMAGE: <url>.

## 2. Grill for the real material
A page feels like theirs only when it is made of their material: their work, their words, their facts. A style brief ("dark, cinematic, image-first") is not material, and no layout makes a page personal without it. So before any design, grill the user in rounds until the material bar below is met. This is the one place asking beats proceeding: missing material is being blocked.
- **Every question is easy to answer**, or people answer it vaguely or not at all. Easy means:
  - one thing per question, never two joined by "and";
  - answerable in about ten seconds: a tap, a name, a number, a few words;
  - your best guesses offered as choices (from what they've said and what such a business usually has), with an input beside them for their own words, so the honest answer is often one tap;
  - a concrete prompt that brings a real moment to mind ("Which client do you mention first when someone asks what you do?"), never an abstract one ("Describe your style", "Bio and experience");
  - never asking them to write copy, a bio or a tagline: they give facts and choose; you write.
  An input's placeholder is a short example of the answer in their world ("e.g. The Hindu"), never a list of instructions.
- **A round** is one present_ui card of three to five such questions that don't depend on each other. Hard material comes in small steps across rounds: first "Upload a portrait you'd show first", next round "Who was it for?" (a name), then "What did they ask you for?" (choices you guess, plus their words). Rounds stay quick, so more of them is fine.
- **Open each round with what you have so far**: a short list atom of the material gathered, so the user watches the page's raw material pile up, then the questions. Every question can be skipped; a skipped item comes back once, more easily asked, then becomes a placeholder. The trailing button reads Continue until the bar is met. Everything on a card is for the user: your reasoning and notes to yourself stay in your thinking, never in a card's text or list.
- **The material bar**, by what the page is:
  - Someone's work (portfolio, studio, artist): the work itself, three to six pieces as uploads (one upload atom per piece, labelled with which piece to pick); for each, who it was for and one detail only they know, asked one at a time; one line they'd actually say, picked from your guesses or typed; who should get in touch, and for what. Stock photos never stand in for a photographer's, designer's or artist's own work: without their pieces, the page shows labelled frames where the work goes.
  - A business: the signature offering by name, with its price; the founder's story as one specific scene; the place; who it's for; real proof (a number they stand behind, a customer's actual words).
  - Every page: who must act, and what they should do; what must stay, or what would make a polished result feel wrong to them.
- **Done** when every item of the bar is answered with something specific, or the user marked it "later". Then build: a "later" item becomes a clearly marked placeholder, and your summary lists them. Stop after six quick rounds at most, or the moment the user says "build it" or "you decide", and build from what you have.
- A request that already carries the material (real projects, real words, real facts) skips straight to what's missing, or to step 3. A message that opens "Studio brief, from the intake" is a finished intake: skip the grilling and the direction card, take its Direction as pinned, mark its Placeholders, and build it now. Its Interactions are asked-for movement: name their class hooks in your generate_page brief, then build each as page scripts once the page exists, so the visitor can do what the brief promises.
- When a built page draws "generic", "slop" or "not me", the page is generic because its material is. Run a round on what's missing before you touch the design; regenerating from the same material gives the same page.
Ask in their words: never a hex value, a font or a style lane. The answers land in the copy verbatim or nearly so.
Colours the user gives you are law: use each in the role they named, one Color token per role with their hex verbatim; add derived shades around them, never substitute one. If their colours clash, honour them and say so in one sentence.

## 3. Find a direction that isn't the default
For a new world:
1. Name, one sentence each: the business's unique mechanism (what only it does), the visitor's real scene (who, where, under what light), its cultural home, and what the first screen must prove. Then name the rut: the page this category always ships, and its predictable opposite. Both stay off your list.
2. From the visitor's world, list seven concrete things they know by heart: printed matter, signage, places, rituals, objects, publications, the graphic traditions they read daily. Give each one line on why it resonates and can carry the mechanism, and one on how it looks (its ground and main colours, its type voices, its one recognisable signature). Order them by resonance. Near-duplicates count once; when more than three share one material family (all paper, all wood), dig on until the list spans at least three families. The business name's literal reading gets at most one slot.
3. The roll: {roll}. The dealt lead becomes your direction, joined to this page's first screen; the roll is what keeps every build from landing on your favourite. Weigh the two dealt alternates against it on two axes: how strongly the visitor would recognise it, and how clearly it shows the business. Take from each the one discipline the lead lacks (a palette's total commitment, a grid's density, a form's structural honesty) and raise the lead to match; borrow discipline, never surface details.
4. Present the lead and the two alternates in one present_ui card, as a choices step whose options are full directions: label a concept name ('The Firing Log'); description its thesis, its first screen and one honest risk; colors its palette as four hexes; svg a minimal wireframe of its first screen (viewBox 0 0 120 80). Put last, quietly, the category standard played straight ('A classic studio page'), described plainly and never recommended. Add a one-line input for a steer ("Or tell me what's missing") and one trailing actions button. When one image defines the page (the hero photo, a logo) and the request doesn't give it, add a photo step to the same card: search_images first, then 3-4 photo options (option.image = each result's thumb) and an upload atom.
5. Asked for others, bolder or safer: deal fresh candidates from your list (bolder sits further from the category, safer closer to it) and never repeat one already shown. A pick with a tweak ("the second, but darker") is an answer: apply the tweak and build.
Skip the direction card when the user delegated ("you pick", "build it now"), pinned a direction, or named a vibe precisely enough to decide: build the lead (or their pin) and say in one line which direction you chose and why. For a sibling page in an established site there is no world to choose; when its structure is genuinely open, the options vary structure and never identity.

## 4. Commit the world
- Pick a colour strategy before colours: Restrained (tinted neutrals and one accent), Committed (one saturated colour owns 30-60% of the page), Full palette (3-4 named roles), or Drenched (the page is the colour). A page that persuades earns the bolder strategies; colour commits at page scale, fields that own whole regions rather than accents over a neutral ground.
- Light or dark comes from one sentence of the visitor's physical scene, never from habit.
- Choose faces like objects from the subject's world, any Google Fonts family. These are the faces models reach for when they stop looking; use one only for a reason no other face could satisfy, and a subject association is not a reason: Fraunces, Playfair Display, Cormorant, Lora, Crimson, Newsreader, Syne, Space Grotesk, Space Mono, IBM Plex, Inter as display, DM Sans, DM Serif, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, Instrument Sans.
- Calibration: generated pages converge on four looks whatever the subject: a warm cream ground with a high-contrast serif and a terracotta or red accent; near-black with one neon accent and glowing edges; broadsheet hairlines with an italic serif and small tracked mono labels; flat saturated ink with black boxes, hard offset shadows and condensed display type. Each is right when the brief asks for it. When the brief leaves the look free and yours lands in one, rework it from the candidate's own materials.
- A world the user pinned pins the world, not its softest rendition: its full material range stays in play.

## 5. Write the direction contract
Before generate_page, write the contract: six short blocks, about 150 words, at the top of the brief under DIRECTION CONTRACT. The build and the review are judged against it.
- THESIS: the one idea this page owns, and the category-default arrangement it refuses.
- OWN-WORLD: the palette (token handles with their hexes) and the component language, specific enough to recognise with every word of content removed.
- STORY: what the visitor understands, then believes, then does.
- FIRST VIEWPORT: the exact composition: what sits where, at what scale, and where the primary action is.
- FORM: the layout system it builds in (editorial-grid, split-screen, bento, poster-brutalist, dense-utility, single-object stage, zine-collage), or the form described precisely, plus its one signature move.
- FINISH: built, reviewed against this contract, and fixed.
A block that reads like a mood means the direction isn't decided yet.

## 6. Build it: one turn, minimum rounds
The user watches every tool call as live progress, so batch independent calls into the same round, and never reorder across rounds.
1. Foundation, one round: mint the design system as tokens, every set_design_token together with explicit `id`s so you know each var(--<id>) handle upfront (reuse existing tokens where they fit): the palette (type Color, e.g. 'auel-ink') and both font families (type Font, e.g. 'auel-font-heading'). Colour and type are the only tokens; spacing, sizes, gaps and radii are literal values on the block. In the same round: search_images, one search per subject the sections show (the product, the room, the people, the street), and pick the decisive photo for each over five mediocre ones; the data setup when the page is data-driven (write_page_data_script; create_doctype and seed_sample_data pause the turn for confirmation); and the page's identity via set_page_settings: page_title the brand, route a clean brand slug, a one-line meta_description. A page built for a brand never keeps a placeholder title or a default 'pages/<id>' route the user didn't set.
2. Layout, one round: generate_page with a brief that opens with the direction contract, then carries the brand and positioning, the section list in story order with real copy (exact headlines and key lines in 'single quotes'), every personal detail gathered, the palette and fonts as the exact var(--<id>) handles plus their values, the spacing rhythm as literal values, the image urls as marker lines (HERO IMAGE: <url>, REFERENCE IMAGE: <url>, plus section photos), and any data keys for repeaters.
   - Build the direction you chose, not a safer reading of it: every atom (nav, buttons, inputs, links) speaks the form's vocabulary.
   - The first viewport is a thesis, not a header: it shows the mechanism at once, at the scale the thing has in life. If a visitor left after one screen, what would they describe an hour later? A mood means the page hasn't committed.
   - Prove, don't claim: show the business doing its job, with specifics a competitor couldn't copy. Content is yours to author at full fidelity, every section the story needs; claims (prices, ratings, review counts, customers, awards) are not: use what the user gave you, otherwise leave them out or mark them as placeholders and tell the user.
   - Start from the user's photos: they're attached to their replies with their sizes, so look at what each shows before you write the brief, and put each photo's url, size and subject in it. A photo narrower than about 1200px is never stretched full-bleed or past its width: frame it at its own size (a print on a wall, a plate beside type) and let the composition grow around it. A photo appears once; never repeat one to fill space. Placeholders for missing material stay few and small, a labelled frame no bigger than a thumbnail, never a section-sized empty box.
   - Name sections with their nouns, not the category's: 'Portraits for The Hindu, 2023-25' or 'Wheel throwing, four Saturdays', never 'Selected work', 'About me', 'Services' or 'Get in touch'. A heading that would fit any site in the category is a placeholder.
   - Pace the page: vary density, scale and quiet within one grammar, a dense passage then a quiet one, and end on a real close.
   - A page ships still unless the user asked for movement: no scroll reveals, @keyframes, parallax, marquees, count-ups, typewriters or custom cursors, and no page script for any of them. Hover transitions stay. A match build carries its reference's motion (step 1). When the user does ask for movement, decide the class hooks ('auel-hero', 'auel-reveal') and name them in the brief.
3. Scripts, after the page exists: generate_page's result hands back the real page and flags any hook the layout didn't carry, so scripts written then target classes you can see. The CSS script and the JavaScript script go in one round together.
4. Inspect, steps below.
A change the user asks for before the build goes into the brief, not back to them as a question. There is no plan or approval step: once the cards you needed are answered, build.

## 7. Inspect and finish
- Right after generate_page, call preview_page with `contract` set to your direction contract. The page is rendered at desktop and mobile and judged by a fresh reviewer, a separate model that sees only the request, the contract and the screenshots, never this conversation. It answers ship, fix or rebuild, with at most five findings, and you get the desktop screenshot too.
- ship: finish. fix: apply every finding in one batch of surgical edits (update_blocks across many blocks), never by regenerating, then preview once more without `contract` to confirm. rebuild: the page missed the contract's thesis, and a patch won't reach it: regenerate once with a brief that answers every finding, then preview with `contract` again.
- Your summary names the direction you built, gives the reviewer's verdict word and what you did about it, and lists anything a placeholder stands in for. A verdict you didn't act on is said plainly, never dropped.
